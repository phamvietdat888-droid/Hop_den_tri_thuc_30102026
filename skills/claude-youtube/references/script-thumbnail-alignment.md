# Script-to-Thumbnail Alignment Framework

Use this reference whenever a video script or transcript is available. The thumbnail is not a summary of the script; it is a compressed promise that must be fulfilled by the title and opening.

## 1. Extract the script's promise

Read the full script before proposing a thumbnail. Extract these fields:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Core conflict | What unfairness, risk, contradiction, or surprising change drives the story? |
| Viewer stake | What could the viewer lose, gain, fear, or hope for? |
| New mechanism | What specific decision, product, policy, evidence, or event changes the situation? |
| Proof anchor | What concrete number, person, place, object, document, or scene makes the promise believable? |
| Uncertainty | What is still proposed, conditional, disputed, or unresolved? |
| Emotional axis | Is the strongest click emotion fear, relief, anger, ambition, disbelief, pride, or curiosity? |

For commentary and policy scripts, prefer the human consequence over abstract institutional language. Translate “a change in compensation policy” into the consequence: “stay near home,” “who pays,” “who loses,” or “can the money buy a new home?”

## 2. Package the information across three surfaces

| Surface | Job |
|---|---|
| Title | State the topic, searchable entity, and concrete promise. |
| Thumbnail | Show the emotional consequence, reversal, conflict, or unanswered question. |
| Opening 5–20 seconds | Immediately prove that the promise is real and explain why it matters now. |

Do not repeat the exact title on the thumbnail. The strongest pair usually has:

`Title = context + topic`  
`Thumbnail = consequence + tension`  
`Hook = concrete scene, number, or paradox`

If the script says “proposal,” “may,” “priority,” or “under discussion,” do not present it as a guaranteed law or universal outcome. Use a question mark or a qualifier when needed. High CTR that creates a promise mismatch is not a successful thumbnail.

## 3. Choose the visual mode

Select one primary mode from the script rather than mixing every idea:

- **Authority + policy reversal**: an identifiable decision-maker or institution, one clear consequence, and a short “new direction” phrase. Best for news, policy, infrastructure, and civic commentary.
- **Personal loss/gain**: a person, home, money, object, or before/after visual that makes the stake tangible.
- **Conflict/question**: two opposing objects or outcomes with a short question such as “AI CHỊU THIỆT?” or “Ở LẠI HAY DỌN ĐI?”.
- **Number/scale**: one dominant number or comparison when money, distance, size, time, or rank is the central story.
- **Transformation**: a clear before/after state when the script promises a visible change.

For policy/commentary thumbnails, a reliable layout is one large subject on the left and a compact two-line text block on the right. The subject should occupy roughly 35–45% of the frame; the text and negative space should occupy the rest. This is a pattern, not a mandatory template.

## 4. Write thumbnail copy from the script

Use the shortest phrase that expresses the script's emotional thesis. Keep each line to 2–5 words; total text can reach 6–7 short words only when it remains instantly readable in two lines.

Useful copy families:

- **Pain**: `TIỀN ĐỀN KHÔNG ĐỦ` / `MẤT NHÀ?`
- **Reversal**: `Ở LẠI HAY DỌN ĐI?` / `BỒI THƯỜNG BẰNG NHÀ?`
- **Conflict**: `AI CHỊU THIỆT?` / `AI ĐƯỢC Ở LẠI?`
- **Novelty**: `ĐỀ XUẤT MỚI` / `NƯỚC CỜ MỚI`
- **Scale**: `67 TỶ USD` / `1.000 KM TÀU ĐIỆN`

Use one dominant phrase and one qualifier. Avoid stacking generic superlatives such as “KHỦNG,” “SỐC,” and “THAY ĐỔI TẤT CẢ” unless the script supplies a specific, defensible scale of change. If a broad phrase is used, the title must anchor it to the real topic.

## 5. Contrast and readability requirements

- Use one dominant focal point, not a collage of faces, arrows, logos, and icons.
- Keep the background darker, simpler, or blurred behind the text.
- Use a warm high-luminance accent (yellow/orange/white) against a dark or cool field; add a dark stroke or shadow to protect legibility.
- Use no more than 2–3 dominant colours plus a neutral outline/background.
- Establish hierarchy: focal subject first, main promise second, qualifier third.
- Use a bold condensed sans-serif or equivalent; all caps are acceptable for short Vietnamese phrases.
- Reserve 30–40% of the frame for breathing room and text clarity; do not fill every corner.
- Inspect at approximately 168×94px. If the face, main phrase, and one context cue do not survive, simplify.

## 6. Promise-fidelity and post-click audit

Before delivery, answer:

1. What exact sentence would a viewer infer from the thumbnail and title together?
2. Does the first 20 seconds deliver evidence for that sentence?
3. Does the script later qualify the claim in a way the packaging hides?
4. Is the thumbnail selling the strongest conflict, or merely the most abstract conclusion?
5. Can the viewer understand the visual thesis without reading the title?

When analytics are available, interpret CTR with context. A high CTR with weak average view duration often means the packaging is strong but the opening or promise delivery needs work. For testing, compare CTR with average view duration, retention, and watch-time share; YouTube's thumbnail experiments optimize for watch-time share, not CTR alone.

## 7. Brief quality score

Score each from 1–5 and explain the weakest score:

- Script relevance
- Emotional clarity
- Curiosity gap
- Mobile legibility
- Visual contrast
- Title–thumbnail complementarity
- Promise fidelity

Do not call a thumbnail “high potential” if promise fidelity or mobile legibility is below 4.
